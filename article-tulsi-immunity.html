Tulsi: Queen of Herbs

Holy Basil (Ocimum sanctum): Millennia of Sacred Worship Backed by Modern Phytochemistry.

Advertisement

Known in Sanskrit as "The Incomparable One", Tulsi (Holy Basil) has stood at the center of Vedic homes for over 5,000 years. Revered as an adaptogenic elixir, Tulsi balances physiological stress, clears pulmonary channels, and elevates mental clarity.

Phytochemical Secrets

Modern clinical research highlights three active compounds in Tulsi leaves:

  • Eugenol: A potent natural compound with antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory properties that soothe throat tissues.
  • Ursolic Acid: A phytochemical shown to protect cellular membranes from oxidative damage and environmental stress.
  • Rosmarinic Acid: A strong antioxidant supporting healthy histamine response and pulmonary circulation.

How to Prepare Traditional Tulsi Tea

1. Take 8–10 fresh organic Tulsi leaves (or 1 tsp dried organic leaves).

2. Boil in 2 cups of pure spring water for 5–7 minutes.

3. Add a small slice of cracked ginger and a drop of raw honey (once warm, never boiling).

4. Drink twice daily during seasonal changes to maintain robust immunity and steady energy levels.
